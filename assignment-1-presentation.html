  6. How does it interact with compatible vehicles?
  7. Why is OpenPilot useful for studying software architecture?
  8. Why does OpenPilot still require driver supervision?

Slides 3–4

Spence Hashemi

Role: System Architecture and Components.

Not Started0% complete

Expected contribution: Slides 3–4, an overall architecture diagram, verified component research, speaker notes, and references.

Jump to a step1. Research2. Components3. Styles4. Architecture slide5. Relationships slide6. Notes7. Submit
Step 1Research the Architecture

Read the OpenPilot repository, official documentation, and current process configuration.

Expected output: Source-backed architecture research notes.

Step 2Identify Major Components

Investigate camera and sensor inputs, perception, planning, control, driver monitoring, vehicle interfaces, user interface, and process management. Examine relevant processes such as modeld, plannerd, controlsd, selfdrived, and dmonitoringd, and verify their current functions in the official repository.

For every component, answer

  • What does it do?
  • What information does it receive?
  • What does it produce?
  • Which components does it communicate with?
  • Why is it a separate component?

Expected output: Verified component inventory with inputs, outputs, responsibilities, and relationships.

Step 3Analyze the Architecture Style

Investigate modular architecture, message-based communication, publish-subscribe interactions, data-processing pipelines, and layered separation of responsibilities. Explain only patterns supported by evidence from the system.

Expected output: Evidence-based architecture-style notes.

Step 4Create Slide 3 – Overall Architecture

Create a simple architecture diagram showing the principal subsystems and their interactions. Use diagrams.net or Mermaid. It must be readable during a presentation.

Expected output: Completed architecture slide and exported diagram.

Step 5Create Slide 4 – Components and Relationships

Explain main subsystem responsibilities, component interactions, architectural organization, communication methods, and important dependencies. Use a compact component table or an additional simplified diagram.

Expected output: Completed components-and-relationships slide.

Step 6Prepare Speaking Notes

Explain the diagram logically, beginning with system inputs and ending with vehicle outputs.

Expected output: Presentation-ready notes for slides 3–4.

Step 7Submit Presentation Materials

Submit slides 3–4, the overall architecture diagram, research references, and speaker notes in editable and integration-ready formats.

Expected output: Complete package ready for team review.

Dedicated Deliverables

  • Slides 3–4.
  • Overall architecture diagram.
  • Research references.
  • Speaker notes.
  • Material ready for integration.

Definition of Done

  • Current component responsibilities are verified.
  • The diagram is readable at presentation size.
  • Architecture claims have source support.
  • Slides and notes are ready for peer review.

Slides 5–6

Mehdi Jafarian

Role: Data Flow, Control Flow, Concurrency, and Sequence Diagram.

Not Started0% complete

Expected contribution: Slides 5–6, a data-flow explanation, adaptive-cruise-control sequence diagram, concurrency explanation, references, and speaker notes.

Jump to a step1. Driving pipeline2. ACC scenario3. Concurrency4. Data-flow slide5. Sequence slide6. Notes7. Submit
Step 1Research the Driving Pipeline

Read the OpenPilot repository, controlsd, and plannerd. Investigate how sensor and perception information is used to generate driving-related control decisions.

Expected output: Verified driving-pipeline research notes.

Step 2Trace an Adaptive Cruise Control Scenario

Study a situation where OpenPilot encounters slower traffic. Investigate how road observations are collected, how surrounding vehicles are represented, how planning responds, how controls determine the desired response, how commands reach the vehicle, and how feedback affects later control decisions. Identify the relevant components and messages.

Expected output: Documented end-to-end ACC scenario.

Step 3Explain Concurrency

Determine which processes operate concurrently, how messages move between them, why concurrency is useful, and what timing considerations exist.

Expected output: Concise concurrency analysis.

Step 4Prepare Slide 5 – Data Flow

Create a clear flow diagram showing how driving information progresses through major subsystems. Explicitly explain the difference between data flow and control flow.

Expected output: Completed data-flow slide and diagram.

Step 5Prepare Slide 6 – Sequence Diagram

Use the scenario Adaptive Cruise Control responding to a slower vehicle. Show the meaningful interactions among participating components. Do not represent publish-subscribe interactions as direct function calls unless the source supports that representation.

Expected output: Completed sequence-diagram slide.

Step 6Prepare Speaking Notes

Explain the sequence from initial observation to control response in plain language while naming the important components and messages accurately.

Expected output: Presentation-ready notes for slides 5–6.

Step 7Submit Materials

Submit slides 5–6, the data-flow explanation, sequence diagram, concurrency explanation, source references, and speaker notes.

Expected output: Complete package ready for team review.

Dedicated Deliverables

  • Slides 5–6.
  • Data-flow explanation.
  • Sequence diagram.
  • Concurrency explanation.
  • References and speaker notes.

Definition of Done

  • ACC flow uses verified processes and messages.
  • Data flow and control flow are distinguished.
  • The sequence diagram models messaging accurately.
  • Slides and notes are ready for peer review.

Adaptive Cruise Control: How does OpenPilot adapt speed to traffic?

Identify which subsystems each use case depends on.
