1Read Requirements

Purpose: Establish the exact scope before writing.

Actions: Read the instructions and Appendix A; list mandatory sections, diagrams, formatting, deadline, publication, and submission rules.

Responsible: Setayesh coordinates; every member confirms their assigned requirements.

Tools & sources: Assignment handout, course instructions, shared outline.

Expected output: Team outline, requirement matrix, and ownership map.

Dependencies: None; this starts the report.

Complete when: Every mandatory element has an owner and planned location.

2Research

Purpose: Build a current, traceable evidence base.

Actions: Read official documentation and relevant source paths; record claims, URLs, questions, assumptions, and source dates.

Responsible: All members research their assigned topics independently.

Tools & sources: OpenPilot documentation, official repository, comma.ai overview, Zotero or shared references.

Expected output: Verified research notes and citations.

Dependencies: The requirement matrix identifies what evidence is needed.

Complete when: Each planned claim and diagram has suitable source support or is marked as an open question.

3Understand Components

Purpose: Establish a common conceptual model and vocabulary.

Actions: Identify major subsystems, responsibilities, inputs, outputs, boundaries, messages, and supporting services.

Responsible: Spence leads; Mehdi and David validate names needed for their analyses.

Tools & sources: Repository layout, process configuration, official documentation, shared glossary.

Expected output: Subsystem inventory and agreed component names.

Dependencies: Current source research.

Complete when: The team agrees on the conceptual subsystem model used across sections and diagrams.

4Analyze Interactions

Purpose: Explain how the system operates across component boundaries.

Actions: Trace data flow, control flow, concurrency, external interfaces, use cases, dependencies, and important architectural decisions.

Responsible: Mehdi leads flow/concurrency; David leads interfaces/use cases; Dan begins trade-off analysis.

Tools & sources: Verified process sources, controls/planning sources, interface sources, subsystem inventory.

Expected output: Source-backed interaction narratives and scenario traces.

Dependencies: Agreed component model from stage 3.

Complete when: Important interactions are documented without inventing direct calls or unsupported behavior.

5Create Diagrams

Purpose: Make structure, dependencies, and behavior understandable.

Actions: Create the architecture overview, dependency diagram, ACC sequence diagram, and driver-monitoring sequence or state diagram; label arrows and add legends.

Responsible: Spence owns architecture/dependency; Mehdi owns sequence 1; David owns sequence/state 2.

Tools & sources: diagrams.net or Mermaid, verified architecture notes.

Expected output: Readable SVG or PNG diagrams plus editable sources.

Dependencies: Component and interaction analysis.

Complete when: Diagrams are technically reviewed, accessible, labeled, cited where needed, and explained in the report.

6Write Sections

Purpose: Convert evidence and diagrams into coherent conceptual analysis.

Actions: Draft assigned sections, explain every diagram, cite sources, state assumptions, and connect each section to the whole system.

Responsible: All members write their assigned contribution.

Tools & sources: Google Docs or Word, research notes, diagrams, shared glossary.

Expected output: Editable section drafts with references and supporting artifacts.

Dependencies: Sufficient research and initial diagrams.

Complete when: Each member's package satisfies their Definition of Done and is ready for review.

7Peer Review

Purpose: Detect technical conflicts, gaps, duplication, and readability problems.

Actions: Cross-check names, relationships, diagrams, citations, conceptual focus, assumptions, and repeated material.

Responsible: All members review another contribution; Dan and Setayesh consolidate issues.

Tools & sources: Shared drafts, comments, issue list, official source links.

Expected output: Reviewed sections, resolved comments, and a remaining-issues list.

Dependencies: Individual drafts and diagrams.

Complete when: Critical technical issues are resolved and ownership is assigned for any remaining edits.

8Integrate Report

Purpose: Turn separate contributions into one unified report.

Actions: Combine sections, normalize headings and terminology, place diagrams, merge references, remove duplication, and check the complete narrative.

Responsible: Setayesh integrates; every member verifies their content after integration.

Tools & sources: Master document, shared glossary, reviewed artifacts, consolidated references.

Expected output: One coherent complete report draft.

Dependencies: Peer-reviewed contributions.

Complete when: Every required section is present, connected, and attributable to reliable sources.

9Format

Purpose: Meet presentation, length, accessibility, and citation requirements.

Actions: Enforce 10–15 pages, single spacing, 12-point font, consistent captions, table styles, headings, references, links, and PDF readability.

Responsible: Setayesh coordinates; all members help shorten or clarify their sections.

Tools & sources: Master document styles, PDF export, accessibility and link checks.

Expected output: Submission-ready report and final PDF candidate.

Dependencies: Integrated report.

Complete when: Formatting rules, page limit, links, citations, and diagram labels pass final review.

10Publish & Submit

Purpose: Deliver a verifiable final artifact before the deadline.

Actions: Export the final PDF, open and inspect it, publish it on the Group H website, verify the public link, and complete the required submission.

Responsible: Setayesh publishes and submits; the team confirms the correct final file.

Tools & sources: PDF viewer, Group H repository, GitHub Pages, course submission system.

Expected output: Public report URL and confirmed submission.

Dependencies: Approved formatted report.

Complete when: The exact final PDF is publicly accessible and submission is confirmed before October 25 at 11:59 PM.

Complete criteria

Report Requirements

The report must focus on conceptual structure and interactions rather than low-level implementation details.

Required content

  • Title and authors.
  • Abstract, approximately two-thirds of a page.
  • Introduction and overview.
  • Conceptual architecture.
  • Major subsystems.
  • Component interactions.
  • Control and data flow.
  • Concurrency.
  • Developer responsibility implications.
  • Overall architecture diagram.
  • Dependency diagram.
  • At least two sequence or state diagrams.
  • External interfaces.
  • Essential use cases.
  • Data dictionary.
  • Naming conventions.
  • Lessons learned.
  • Conclusions.
  • References.

Step 1Study the Architecture

Use the OpenPilot repository, official documentation, and current process configuration. Examine how OpenPilot organizes functionality into processes and modules.

Expected output: Source-backed architecture notes and current process inventory.

Step 2Identify Major Subsystems

Investigate camera and sensor inputs; perception and modeld; planning and plannerd; control and controlsd; driver monitoring; vehicle interface, opendbc, and panda; user interface; process management; and supporting services.

For every subsystem, answer

  • What is its purpose?
  • What does it receive?
  • What does it produce?
  • Which subsystems does it interact with?
  • Why is this separation useful?

Expected output: Subsystem inventory table containing responsibilities, inputs, outputs, dependencies, and references.

Step 3Analyze Architectural Styles

Investigate modular architecture, message-based communication, publish-subscribe communication, data-processing pipelines, and layered responsibilities. Explain which styles are supported by evidence and where they apply.

Expected output: Evidence-based architectural-style analysis.

Step 4Create the Overall Architecture Diagram

Use diagrams.net or Mermaid. Include the major subsystems and meaningful relationships, label all connections, and retain an editable source file.

Expected output: Architecture diagram in SVG or PNG format.

Step 5Create the Dependency Diagram

Show dependencies between modules or subsystems, make dependency direction clear, and explain arrows and symbols with a legend. Do not confuse dependencies with data flow.

Expected output: Dependency diagram with legend.

Step 6Write the Report Section

Explain the major subsystems, responsibilities, interactions, architectural styles, dependencies, and important strengths and limitations. Explain both diagrams in the text and cite technical claims.

Expected output: Completed 2–3 page section, both diagrams, editable sources, references, and unresolved questions.

  • Editable 2–3 page report section.
  • Subsystem inventory.
  • Architecture diagram and editable source.
  • Dependency diagram and legend.
  • References, assumptions, and system relationship note.

Definition of Done

  • Subsystem responsibilities and boundaries are sourced.
  • Architecture styles are evidence-based.
  • Both diagrams are labeled and explained.
  • Dependencies are distinct from data flow.
  • The package is ready for peer review.

Verify terms such as modelV2, carState, carControl, longitudinalPlan, driverMonitoringState, selfdriveState, controlsState, radarState, CAN, and panda using official project sources. Use only confirmed terms.
