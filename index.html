Setayesh CheginiProject Coordinator, Introduction, Abstract & Final Integration 0% complete

Expected contribution: Approximately 2 pages of original writing, plus final report integration and publication.

Start with official sources:OpenPilot documentationOpenPilot repositoryOpenPilot overview
  1. 1

    Planning

    Understand the Assignment

    • Read all assignment instructions and Appendix A.
    • List every mandatory report section and required diagram.
    • Confirm the 10–15 page limit, single spacing, and 12-point font.
    • Create a common document outline for the team.

    Output: Complete report outline and requirements checklist.

  2. 2

    Research

    Research OpenPilot's Purpose

    Determine what OpenPilot is, which driver-assistance problems it addresses, its principal features, hardware and vehicle interfaces, limitations, and how it differs from full autonomy.

    Output: Research notes covering purpose, functionality, and limitations.

  3. 3

    Writing

    Write the Introduction

    • Define OpenPilot and explain why its architecture is worth studying.
    • Summarize its major functionality.
    • State the report's scope and objectives.
    • Explain how the remaining report is organized.

    Output: Approximately 1–1.5 pages of introduction and overview.

  4. 4

    Writing

    Write the Abstract

    After all other sections are drafted, summarize the study purpose, major subsystems, interactions, architectural findings, and conclusions.

    Output: Approximately two-thirds of a page.

  5. 5

    Integration & review

    Integrate the Final Report

    • Collect every contribution, diagram, and reference.
    • Check required sections, consistent terminology, labels, legends, and citations.
    • Ensure each diagram is explained and repeated material is removed.
    • Keep the report focused on conceptual architecture.

    Output: One coherent, reviewed 10–15 page report.

  6. 6

    Submission

    Complete Final Publication

    • Export and verify the final report PDF.
    • Upload it to the Group H website and test every link.
    • Collect or prepare presentation slides.
    • Export the slides as a submission-ready PDF.

    Output: Published report link and presentation PDF.

Definition of Done

  • Introduction and abstract are complete.
  • The integrated report satisfies all requirements.
  • The publication link has been verified.
  • The presentation PDF is ready.
Spence HashemiSystem Architecture, Subsystems & Dependency Diagrams 0% complete

Expected contribution: 2–3 pages, including the architecture and dependency diagrams.

Start with official sources:OpenPilot repositoryOpenPilot documentationProcess configuration
  1. 1

    Research

    Study the Overall Architecture

    Examine how OpenPilot divides functionality among processes and modules. Record reliable source links for every claim.

    Output: Initial architecture notes and process list.

  2. 2

    Analysis

    Identify the Major Subsystems

    Cover camera and sensor inputs, perception, planning, control, driver monitoring, vehicle interface, user interface, process management, and supporting services.

    • For each subsystem, document its responsibility, inputs, outputs, interactions, and reason for separation.

    Output: Subsystem inventory table with dependencies and source references.

  3. 3

    Analysis

    Identify the Architecture Styles

    Evaluate evidence for modular, message-based or publish-subscribe, pipeline, and layered patterns. Explain where each pattern applies without forcing the whole system into one style.

    Output: Evidence-based architecture-style analysis.

  4. 4

    Diagram

    Create the Overall Architecture Diagram

    Show camera inputs, perception, planning, control, driver monitoring, vehicle integration, and user-facing outputs with clear, labeled information flows.

    Output: Conceptual architecture diagram in SVG or PNG format.

  5. 5

    Diagram

    Create the Dependency Diagram

    Show dependency direction and include a legend explaining that a dependency from M to N means M requires information or services from N. Do not present a data-flow diagram as a dependency diagram.

    Output: Dependency diagram in SVG or PNG format with a legend.

  6. 6

    Writing

    Write the Architecture Section

    Explain the subsystems, responsibilities, interactions, architecture styles, dependency relationships, and the benefits and limitations of this organization.

    Output: A referenced 2–3 page section plus both diagrams.

Definition of Done

  • The architecture section is 2–3 pages.
  • The subsystem inventory is supported by sources.
  • Architecture and dependency diagrams are labeled and exported.
  • References are included and work is ready for review.
Mehdi JafarianControl Flow, Data Flow, Concurrency & Sequence Diagram 1 0% complete

Expected contribution: 2–3 pages, including the adaptive-cruise-control sequence diagram.

Study these sources:Process configurationcontrolsdplannerdprocess replay
  1. 1

    Research

    Study How Data Moves

    Investigate OpenPilot's message-based communication and identify the processes and messages relevant to driving control.

    Output: Source-backed notes on processes, messages, publishers, and subscribers.

  2. 2

    Data flow

    Trace the Driving Pipeline

    Trace an adaptive-cruise-control scenario from road and vehicle observations through perception, planning, controls, the vehicle interface, and feedback from updated vehicle state.

    Output: Documented end-to-end data-flow explanation with actual processes and messages.

  3. 3

    Control flow

    Explain System Operation

    Explain startup, process activation, engagement, command delivery, disengagement, and reactions to unavailable or invalid inputs. Clearly separate verified behavior from assumptions.

    Output: Evidence-based control-flow analysis.

  4. 4

    Concurrency

    Investigate Concurrent Processing

    Identify separate processes, concurrent activities, message-based coordination, real-time benefits, and conceptual handling of delayed, missing, or invalid messages.

    Output: Short concurrency analysis.

  5. 5

    Diagram

    Create Sequence Diagram 1

    Model adaptive cruise control responding to a slower vehicle. Include sensors, camera processing, perception, radar where applicable, longitudinal planning, controls, vehicle interface, and vehicle. Show publish-subscribe messages instead of implying every interaction is a direct function call.

    Output: Verified sequence diagram in SVG or PNG format.

  6. 6

    Writing

    Write the Report Section

    Explain data flow, control flow, concurrency, the sequence diagram, and the source evidence used.

    Output: A referenced 2–3 page analysis and one sequence diagram.

Definition of Done

  • The data and control flows use verified processes and messages.
  • Concurrency is explained conceptually.
  • Sequence diagram 1 is labeled and exported.
  • The 2–3 page section and references are ready for review.

Dan LogvinovskyDesign Analysis, Developer Responsibilities, Data Dictionary & Conclusions 0% complete

Expected contribution: Approximately 2–3 pages including supporting material, adjusted to keep the final report within 15 pages.

Study these sources:OpenPilot repositoryProcess replay guideSafety documentation
  1. 1

    Review

    Review the System Architecture

    Study the architecture material produced by Spence, Mehdi, and David and identify the design decisions that shape OpenPilot.

    Output: Consolidated architecture-review notes.

  2. 2

    Design analysis

    Investigate Architectural Trade-offs

    Analyze process separation, message-based communication, real-time performance, fault handling, reliability, maintainability, extensibility, safety boundaries, and support for different vehicles. State advantages, disadvantages, and implications.

    Output: Architectural trade-off analysis.

  3. 3

    Development

    Explain Developer Responsibilities

    Explain how contributors can work across perception, planning, controls, vehicle integration, monitoring, UI, testing, integration, and safety. Identify shared interfaces and contracts without inventing OpenPilot's internal employee structure.

    Output: Developer-responsibility discussion clearly labeled as architecture analysis.

  4. 4

    Reference material

    Create the Data Dictionary

    Verify and document terms such as modelV2, carState, carControl, longitudinalPlan, driverMonitoringState, selfdriveState, controlsState, radarState, CAN, and panda.

    Output: Table with Term, Type/Category, Meaning, and Used By columns.

  5. 5

    Reference material

    Document Naming Conventions

    Explain subsystem and process names, background processes ending in d, message and data-structure names, module naming, and important abbreviations.

    Output: Concise naming-convention guide.

  6. 6

    Reliability

    Investigate Testing and Safety

    Use the process-replay and safety documentation to explain how automated tests, replay, and safety mechanisms support the architecture without drifting into detailed test implementation.

    Output: Short testing-and-reliability analysis.

  7. 7

    Writing

    Write Lessons Learned and Conclusions

    Summarize architectural strengths, limitations, analysis challenges, lessons learned, possible study improvements, and future development directions.

    Output: Lessons-learned and conclusion section.

  8. 8

    Integration

    Organize References

    Collect all members' sources, remove duplicates, confirm working URLs, and apply one consistent citation format.

    Output: Consolidated reference list.

Definition of Done

  • Trade-offs and developer responsibilities are explained.
  • The data dictionary and naming guide use verified terminology.
  • Testing, safety, lessons learned, and conclusions are complete.
  • References are consolidated and the material is ready for integration.

Assignment criteria

Assignment 1 Report Requirements

  • Final report length: 10–15 pages.
  • Format: Single-spaced, 12-point font.
  • Focus on conceptual architecture, not implementation details.
  • Include at least two sequence diagrams or state diagrams.
  • Include an architecture overview diagram.
  • Include a dependency diagram.
  • Explain the main subsystems and their interactions.
  • Describe control flow, data flow, and concurrency.
  • Explain how responsibilities are divided among participating developers.
  • Provide proper references and relevant external links.
  • Publish the final report on the Group H website.
  • Submit presentation slides in PDF format before the class presentation.
